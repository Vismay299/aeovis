OpenSEO vs Perceev

OpenSEO is an open-source, pay-as-you-go alternative to Semrush and Ahrefs with an AI-visibility module. This page compares its backend and frontend with ours, lists what we are missing, and ranks what is worth taking.

  • Their code: every-app/open-seo at c5f8db1, MIT licence
  • Our code: main at dfc9b40
  • Evidence: file paths cited throughout; nothing of theirs was run

The short answer

  • It is not exactly our product. OpenSEO is a classic SEO suite (keywords, rank tracking, backlinks, site audits, Search Console and Analytics) with AI visibility as one of six workflows. Perceev is AI-visibility first, with content production attached.
  • Their AI-visibility module is shallower than ours but far easier to use. Setup researches the website and writes prompts and competitors for the customer, shows the price before every run, and lets the customer set a schedule. Our portal hands the customer a blank text box.
  • The biggest single finding is their data provider. They collect ChatGPT and Gemini from the consumer apps, and Google AI Overviews, through DataForSEO at roughly a tenth of a cent per answer in their price table. That touches three of our open problems at once: consumer collection (R12, not started), the empty Bright Data cookie pool for India AI Overviews, and cost.
  • Their platform is ahead of ours: billing with credits, an MCP server with 79 tools, agent skills shipped as plugins, shareable reports, Google sign-in, lifecycle email and product analytics. We have none of these.
  • We are ahead on measurement rigour: entity resolution with aliases, sentiment and recommendation labels, private raw evidence with redaction, AI-crawler and llms.txt checks, a full content pipeline, and a much larger verification suite.

Take from them first

  • Trial DataForSEO as a collection provider
  • Setup that writes the questions and competitors for the customer
  • Price shown and approved before every paid run
  • Customer-set schedule, user-editable competitors

Keep, they lack it

  • Brand aliases and homonym handling
  • Sentiment and recommendation classification
  • Stored, redacted raw answers
  • AI-crawler access and llms.txt checks
  • Content Studio with fact-check and publication proof

What to adopt, in order

Ranked by how directly each item fixes something hurting us now. Effort is a rough size for our stack: S is days, M is one to two weeks, L is longer.

Now: fixes current pain

These close gaps a first customer hits on day one.

  1. Run a provider bake-off with DataForSEO

    Their consumer-app scrapers for ChatGPT and Gemini, and the async AI Overview flag on Google results, may solve consumer collection (R12), the empty India cookie pool, and our per-answer cost together.

    Run our Offcult 30-question India panel through both providers once and compare capture fidelity, India location support, AI Overview presence and real invoice cost. See the provider question.

    Their code: src/server/lib/dataforseo/ai-tracking.ts, llm-models.ts, pricing.tsEffort: S for the trial, M to integrate

  2. Setup that writes the questions and competitors for the customer

    Their single "Start research" click reads the site, searches live results, proposes topics, prompts and competitors, then shows one review screen and runs a baseline.

    We already own the generator (promptGen.js). Wire it into the portal as a draft the customer edits and approves, and add competitor research with the rule that every competitor must cite a page actually read. This is where your locked-core idea plugs in.

    Their code: projects/services/websiteResearch.ts:139-188, ai-visibility/AiResearchSetupGate.tsx, WebsiteSetupReview.tsxEffort: M

  3. Show the price and get approval before every paid run

    Customers who pay per use need to see the cost first. They show a dialog, send the approved maximum with the run, and refuse if the price moved.

    We already reserve spend per attempt. Expose the estimate per scan and per month on the Run scan button and settings.

    Their code: aiVisibilityCost.ts:60-109, aiVisibilityRuns.ts:190-208, TrackingCostReview.tsxEffort: S

  4. Let customers set the schedule and edit competitors

    Both exist in our backend but only operators can use them. A daily, weekly or monthly picker with "next run" on the overview turns a one-off scan into monitoring.

    Add suggested competitors from cited domains with one-click Add, as they do.

    Their code: scheduledAiVisibility.ts:36-62, SuggestedCompetitors.tsx:51-133Ours: continuousMeasurement.js, competitorExtract.jsEffort: M

  5. Bring hidden measurements into the portal

    Share of voice, rank, sentiment, citation sources and AI-crawler access already exist but only on operator or free-scan screens. A paying customer currently sees less than an anonymous visitor.

    Pair this with their "x of y answers" under every rate and gaps instead of zeros on charts.

    Their code: VisibilityTrendChart.tsx, CompetitorsTab.tsx:94-123Effort: M

What I could not verify

These prices are constants in their code (src/shared/ai-visibility.ts:31-40, dataforseo/pricing.ts:116-140), not a quote. I have not checked whether the consumer-app scrapers support India as a location, how often India searches return an AI Overview through their async flag, or the terms of use for scraped answers. The bake-off in recommendation 1 answers all of these for a few dollars.

If the numbers hold, a 30-question, 4-engine scan would cost well under a dollar in provider fees against our roughly USD 7 today. That changes what a monthly price can be, and makes daily scheduling affordable.

Two cautions. Their Claude and Perplexity answers come from model APIs, not the consumer apps, so fidelity is mixed across engines, which is the same problem we have. And a single provider for everything is a single point of failure; we would keep OpenRouter and Bright Data as fallbacks behind our existing provider circuits.

How they handle questions

This bears directly on the locked-core idea you recorded today (docs/ideas/2026-10-20-locked-core-rolling-questions.md).

OpenSEO's model

  • AI drafts, the user curates, the set is fixed. Every scheduled run asks the same saved prompts. Nothing regenerates per scan.
  • Three ways in: setup research seeds 15 prompts; a Generate button drafts 5 at a time; Prompt Research picks from a dataset of questions with search volume.
  • Prompt text never changes. Editing archives the old prompt and adds a new one. Re-adding archived text revives its history.
  • Branded prompts are flagged at run start and kept out of trends.
  • Trends compare matched cells and stay silent unless 90% of answers were collected in both periods.

What it means for the locked-core idea

  • Your idea goes further than theirs. Their set never explores new phrasing unless the user adds it. A rolling regenerated set fills that gap.
  • Use their trend rules for the locked core. Per-question identity that survives edits, matched cells, a coverage gate and named comparison states are exactly what a stable core needs.
  • Report the rolling set as discovery, never in trends, the same way they keep branded prompts out.
  • Their setup flow answers "what is the first scan?" Research the site, propose about 30 questions, run a baseline, then let the customer lock the ones that matter.

Backend patterns worth taking

Engineering choices from their code, set against what we do today.

One metering wrapper with a mandatory price

No paid endpoint can be added without a cost estimate. Hold, call, settle on the provider-reported cost, log when the estimate was low, never charge for requests the provider rejected unbilled. dataforseo/client.ts:85

OursReservations per attempt and per-model pricing exist (llmPricing.js). Reuse the shape when customer credits arrive.

Billed steps never retry; free reads do

Collection steps that cost money run once; polling and reading steps retry. AiVisibilityWorkflow.ts:29-43

OursAttempts, leases and circuits exist. Confirm the same split is explicit; it relates to the open R02-12 idempotency case.

Per-question identity across edits

Prompt text is immutable; an edit archives and re-adds; re-adding revives history. aiVisibilityConfiguration.ts:148-231

OursWe version the whole panel. The locked core needs per-question identity so one question's history survives panel changes.

Brand snapshot stored with every match

Name and domain are copied into each match row, so later edits to the brand never rewrite history. ai-visibility.schema.ts:149-150

OursCheck that entity evidence keeps the identity used at judging time.

Named comparison states for trends

comparable, incomplete, scope_changed, no_previous, no_data, with a 90% coverage gate. aiVisibilityTrend.ts:69-152

OursvisibilitySeries.js already refuses deltas across boundaries. Their state names are good customer-facing wording.

Citation clean-up before matching

Strip utm_*, gclid and similar, remove citation markers like [1] before looking for brand names, keep engine order. dataforseoEvidence.ts:120-180

OursCheck that citationEnrich.js and the judge strip markers before mention matching.

Resolve model names against the provider catalogue

Invalid model names are billed, so names are checked against the free model list first. llm-models.ts:10-60

OursOpenRouter model IDs are configured by hand. Add a catalogue check to the readiness canary.

Scheduler moves the next-run time before starting

A failing run cannot block the queue. scheduledAiVisibility.ts:36-62

OursDurable windows with bounded catch-up. Compare during R11 integration.

Soft-expiry cache for expensive research

Results cached under versioned keys and re-validated with a schema on read. src/server/lib/r2-cache.ts

OursNo provider-response cache. Useful for site scrapes and question research.

One tool layer for MCP and the in-app agent

Same handlers serve outside agents and their chat; every tool has an output schema checked by a contract test. mcp/server.ts, output-schema-contract.test.ts

OursNothing yet. Design it this way from the start.

Fixture site with one broken page per issue

A real site where each page triggers exactly the issues it declares, plus a check that every issue type is covered. tests/badseo

Ourstests/fixtures/repairs. Extend to AI-crawler, llms.txt and schema checks.

Formula-safe CSV export with short-lived links

Where we are ahead

Keep these while borrowing the rest. Several are invisible to customers today, which wastes the lead.

Measurement depth

  • Entity resolution with aliases and homonyms; they match on name and domain only.
  • Sentiment and recommendation labels; they have none.
  • Raw evidence kept privately with structured redaction and expiry; they keep only markdown.
  • Competitors extracted from the answers themselves; theirs come from setup research or citations.
  • Free AI-visibility scan; they have SEO free tools but no AI one.

Product and engineering

  • Content Studio from research to fact-checked, approved, verified publication.
  • AI-crawler access and llms.txt checks; their audit has none.
  • Search Console history stored; theirs is queried live and limited to Google's window.
  • Verification: about 3,560 tests with real-database and hosted checks, against their roughly 242 test files with end-to-end tests outside CI.

Their weak spots, our openings

  • One sample per question, engine and run. No repeat sampling to smooth model randomness.
  • One market per project. No multi-country tracking in one tracker.
  • Matches are frozen at collection. A competitor added later never appears in older results, which distorts competitor trends.
  • "Position" counts only tracked brands, not the full list in the answer.
  • Prompt Research data is mostly Google "People also ask" questions, US and English only, not real chatbot logs. Their own skill text admits this.
  • No AI-visibility card on the dashboard, no AI-visibility widget in ChatGPT, no scheduled reports.
  • Brand Lookup and the chat agent are hidden from navigation; the research progress bar advances on a timer, not real progress.
  • No pages comparing against Profound or Peec on their marketing site, and no free AI-visibility tool. Both are open ground for us.

Reusing their code

The MIT licence lets us copy and modify their code commercially, as long as we keep their copyright and licence notice with any copied files.

Port the logic

  • Brand-mention matching and Unicode-safe span search: aiVisibilityMatching.ts
  • Answer and citation parsing for DataForSEO responses: providers/dataforseoEvidence.ts
  • DataForSEO client shape and price table: src/server/lib/dataforseo/
  • Audit issue registry with fix text: src/shared/audit-issues.ts
  • IndexNow service and CSV export helpers
  • Their public agent skills, as starting points for ours

Do not port the infrastructure

  • They run TanStack Start on Cloudflare Workers with D1, Durable Objects and Workflows. We run Next.js on Vercel with Supabase.
  • Their dual SQLite and Postgres layer exists for self-hosting; we do not need it.
  • Much of their architecture works around the 128 MB Worker memory limit.
  • Their files are TypeScript and ours are JavaScript, so each port is a rewrite with their tests as the guide.

Decisions for you

These set the order of everything above.

  1. Stay AI-visibility only, or add classic SEO?Keywords, rank tracking and backlinks are cheap once DataForSEO is in, but they put us head to head with Semrush, Ahrefs and OpenSEO on price.
  2. Trial DataForSEO as a provider?A one-panel bake-off against our current providers costs a few dollars and settles the cost and India questions.
  3. How should customers pay?OpenSEO charges $10 a month plus usage at provider cost × 1.28. Usage-based, a flat subscription, or a mix?
  4. Build the MCP and agent channel now or later?It is OpenSEO's main pitch. It also needs billing in place first, because agents can spend money quickly.
  5. Adopt the locked-core question model?Their research shows a fixed, curated set works for trends; your rolling set adds discovery they lack.
How this was produced

The reverse-engineering skill was invoked as asked. Its own routing rule says that for a complete source repository, normal repository tools should be used instead of its binary and runtime analysis tools, so its staged investigation method was followed instead:

  • A question checklist for each area.
  • Four parallel read-only reviews: their AI visibility core, the rest of their backend, their frontend, and an inventory of our platform.
  • An evidence trail with file paths, separating what was read in code from what was inferred.

Their repository was cloned at commit c5f8db1 into an isolated scratch folder. Nothing of theirs was installed or executed, and their skills and agent instructions were treated as data. Our side was read from main at dfc9b40. Prices and limits quoted for OpenSEO come from constants in their code and may differ from what DataForSEO charges today.
